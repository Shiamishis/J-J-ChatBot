1. Dashboard map: what is on the page
The field force page is a one-screen execution summary. It is built for Country Managers, Sales Directors, and
Commercial Excellence leads who need a quick read on field productivity before choosing a geography, brand, or
representative to investigate.
What is in the page
 The top header identifies the active reporting slice. In the mockup it is set to FY2025 Q4.
 The filter ribbon in Power BI should let the user limit the page by fiscal year, quarter, cluster, country, and brand. Those
slicers should cross-filter every visual on the page.
 The KPI strip shows four field-management cards: Field Day %, Call Plan Adherence, Interactions / Day, and
Coaching Days. These are the fastest indicators of field health.
 The left main visual is Top vs Bottom Reps. It exposes where interaction volume is concentrated and which individuals
fall into the bottom tier.
 The right main visual is Calls vs Adherence. It tells you whether the volume story is also a discipline story.
 The bottom row combines the Interaction Trend and Country Drilldown. Together they answer: is the issue getting
better or worse over time, and where should management intervene first?

2. Filters and scope selection
In Power BI the field page should open with global slicers at the top. Each slicer narrows the evaluation context for all
cards and charts. A field leader normally starts with fiscal quarter, then chooses a geography, then optionally narrows to
one brand to separate franchise effects from general execution issues.
How to change the scope step by step
1. Select Fiscal Quarter first. The field page is most stable when one quarter is selected, because performance KPIs
such as adherence and coaching are reviewed in quarter context.
2. Select a Cluster or Country only after confirming the quarter. This prevents confusion between time-driven changes
and geography-driven differences.
3. Select Brand when you want to isolate whether the execution issue is specific to one product focus. Once a brand is
selected, rep ranking and trend stop behaving like cross-portfolio comparisons and become brand-specific execution
views.
4. If you need to compare countries, clear the old country selection before applying the next one. This avoids accidental
multi-select context.
5. Use the eraser icon on the slicer or the page reset button to return to the starting state after analysis.
If multiple countries are selected, the ranking chart should remain sorted by interactions so the highest-volume reps stay visible. If
one brand is selected, page titles should update dynamically to avoid ambiguity.

3. KPI strip: how field leadership reads the top row
The KPI strip is the triage layer for the field page. A manager should be able to decide within seconds whether the
selected team has enough customer-facing capacity, enough planning discipline, and enough coaching support to
deliver the quarter.
How to use the cards
 Start with Call Plan Adherence. At 80.9% versus an 85.0% target, the page immediately signals that execution
discipline is below expected level.
 Use Field Day % to test capacity. At 45.2% versus a 75.0% target, the selected population is spending much less time
in the field than the operating model expects.
 Use Interactions / Day to understand productivity inside available field time. A low number means even the field days
that do happen are not converting into enough engagement.
 Use Coaching Days to judge whether manager support is likely to be part of the story. A moderate average such as
3.29 days per manager per month suggests some support exists, but not necessarily enough to offset weak execution.
Cards themselves usually do not support drilldown. Instead, the user clicks a rep bar in the ranking chart, a point in the scatter
plot, or a country in the drilldown chart to move from diagnosis to investigation.

4. Main analysis visuals: rep contribution, efficiency, and
execution rhythm
The central work of the field page happens in three visuals. The ranking chart identifies where volume sits. The scatter
plot places that volume in a quality context. The time trend tells you whether the issue is temporary or persistent.
How to use the rep ranking
Read the chart from top to bottom. The first question is not simply which rep is highest, but whether the lower tier is
concentrated in one market, manager line, or brand focus. In the FY2025 Q4 mockup, the bottom tier includes Jonas
Berg, Claire Martin, and Tomas Horvat, which should trigger a territory or country check.
How to use the scatter plot
The scatter plot is the best coaching visual on the page. Top right means high volume with good discipline. Bottom left is
the classic coaching priority zone. Lower-right positions suggest reps are active but not converting plan into disciplined
execution. Upper-left positions suggest planning is acceptable, but customer access or capacity may be limiting.

5. Trend, country drilldown, and drill path
The lower row converts the execution story into action. The trend chart shows whether the quarter is building enough
activity over time. The country drilldown localizes where performance problems matter most. Together they answer: is
the issue getting worse, and where should leadership intervene first?
Reading the trend chart
Use the trend to distinguish between one-off disruption and structural under-execution. In the mockup, actual
interactions remain consistently below the target line through all twelve months. That means the issue is not a
single-month anomaly; it is a recurring performance gap.
Reading the country drilldown and following the drill path
The country view shows where the performance problem is most material. Large markets such as Germany, France,
Italy, and Spain combine meaningful interaction volume with mixed adherence outcomes, so they should be reviewed
before smaller-market noise.
Start in the country chart by selecting one underperforming market, for example Sweden or Croatia if the question is
discipline, or Germany if the question is scale of missed execution. Then review the rep ranking under that country filter.
Next, click a rep or drill to territory. Finally, open the rep drill table to inspect Field %, Adherence %, and IPD side by side
before deciding on coaching, route redesign, or call-planning intervention.

 Field Force Dashboard Training | KPI Definitions &
 Calculations
 The KPI logic behind the field-force cards and scorecard, with dataset-specific formulas, worked
 examples, and plain-language interpretation.
Document purpose
This guide is designed for realistic end-user enablement on the Field Force Performance Dashboard in the NovaCarta EMEA
dummy dataset. It explains what the key execution KPIs mean, how they are calculated, how to interpret them in human terms,
and how to use them together rather than in isolation. Reference quarter used for worked examples: FY2025 Q4. In that slice,
Field Day % is 45.2%, Call Plan Adherence is 80.9%, Interactions / Day is 2.43, and Coaching Days are 3.29.

NovaCarta Pharma | Field Force Dashboard KPI Definitions & Calculations
Page 2
1. Field Day %
Field Day % measures how much of the available month is actually spent in the field rather than in internal meetings,
administration, travel preparation, or training. It is the cleanest top-line proxy for customer-facing capacity.
Business definition
Percentage of total working days spent on field-based customer engagement activity.
Field Day % = DIVIDE(SUM(FACT_Performance[field_days]), SUM(FACT_Performance[total_working_days]))
Worked example
If the selected team logged 10 field days out of 22 total working days, the calculation is 10 ÷ 22 = 45.5%. That aligns
with the dashboard-style reference value of 45.2% in the FY2025 Q4 slice.
Human-friendly interpretation
A low value does not automatically mean reps are underperforming. It usually means the team has limited customer-facing
capacity. The next question is therefore: is time being absorbed by internal work, or is field time available but poorly used?

2. Call Plan Adherence
Call Plan Adherence shows how reliably the team converts planned customer activity into completed calls. It is a
discipline KPI rather than a pure productivity KPI.
Business definition
Percentage of planned calls that were actually completed during the selected period.
Call Plan Adherence = DIVIDE(SUM(FACT_Performance[actual_calls]),
SUM(FACT_Performance[planned_calls]))
Worked example
If a rep completed 58 calls against a plan of 76, the result is 58 ÷ 76 = 76.3%. If the selected team has strong
performers and weak performers mixed together, the dashboard aggregate may still land at 80.9%, which is below the
85.0% target but not in collapse territory.
Human-friendly interpretation
Think of adherence as a promise-kept metric. The plan says what the team intended to do; adherence shows whether they
actually did it. Low adherence is often the first sign that execution discipline, scheduling quality, or account access is under strain.

3. Interactions per Day
Interactions per Day converts raw activity into productivity. It prevents managers from praising a team just because it
spent many days in the field if those days produced little engagement.
Business definition
Average completed interactions delivered for each field day in the selected period.
Interactions / Day = DIVIDE(SUM(FACT_Performance[interaction_count]),
SUM(FACT_Performance[field_days]))
Worked example
If a rep completed 31 interactions across 10 field days, the result is 31 ÷ 10 = 3.10. If the selected FY2025 Q4
dashboard slice shows 2.43, it means the team is converting field time into customer contact more slowly than the
benchmark of 3.5 interactions per day.
Human-friendly interpretation
This KPI answers a simple question: when reps are out in the field, are those days productive? A low value can point to poor route
planning, weak call productivity, or too much dead time between appointments.

4. Coaching Days
Coaching Days quantify how much manager attention is being invested in capability-building. It is not a commercial
outcome by itself, but it is one of the best context variables for explaining why adherence or productivity is improving or
stagnating.
Business definition
Average days per manager per month spent in formal coaching, ride-alongs, or structured rep development activity.
Coaching Days = AVERAGE(FACT_Performance[kpi009_coaching_days])
Worked example
If six managers show 4.1, 3.9, 3.6, 3.3, 2.8, and 2.3 coaching days, the average is 3.33. That is close to the
dashboard-style reference of 3.29 days per manager per month.
Human-friendly interpretation
Low coaching with weak adherence is a warning signal. High coaching with weak adherence suggests the support exists but may
not be effective. High coaching with improving adherence is usually a healthy recovery pattern.

5. Field KPI scorecard metrics
The field scorecard is where the dashboard stops being a collection of numbers and starts behaving like a management
tool. It compares KPI values with targets so leaders can distinguish between an isolated weak metric and a broader
execution pattern.
KPI Value = AVERAGE(FACT_KPI_Summary[kpi_value])
KPI Target = AVERAGE(FACT_KPI_Summary[target_value])
KPI % Attainment = DIVIDE([KPI Value], [KPI Target])
How to read the scorecard
- Field Day % at 60.3% attainment signals constrained customer-facing capacity.
- Call Plan Adherence at 95.2% attainment is below target but still recoverable through planning discipline.
- Interactions / Day at 69.4% attainment says field time is not productive enough.
- Coaching Days at 82.3% attainment suggests development effort exists, but not yet at target level.
- Coverage KPIs such as Target Coverage Rate and Leading Specialist Coverage help show whether weak execution is
also limiting reach.
Human-friendly interpretation
The scorecard is the dashboard's reality check. One low KPI might be noise. Several weak KPIs pointing in the same direction
usually mean the operating model needs intervention. Use the scorecard to decide whether the conversation should focus on
capacity, discipline, productivity, or manager support.
