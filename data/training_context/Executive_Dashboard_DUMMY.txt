 Decision-Making Playbook
How to move from a red executive page to a prioritized action plan using filters, drilldowns, and KPI
interpretation.
Document purpose
This guide is designed for realistic end-user enablement on the Executive Performance Dashboard in the
NovaCarta EMEA dummy dataset. It explains what each area of the dashboard contains, how to filter and drill
into data, how the executive KPIs are calculated, and how to turn those signals into actions.
Reference quarter used for worked examples: FY2025 Q4. In that slice, total revenue is €3.70B, target
revenue is €6.08B, weighted market share is 19.49%, and weighted YoY growth is 19.62%.

1. First five clicks: a disciplined start to analysis
1. Set the fiscal quarter. 2. Read the KPI strip. 3. Scan the brand chart. 4. Read the market share trend. 5.
Read the country heat table only after the portfolio story is clear.
Starting with country first can lead to random hunting. Starting with the portfolio cards and brand chart
preserves executive discipline: first size the problem, then isolate the drivers, then localize ownership.

2. Decision pattern: when one brand drives most of the
miss
In FY2025 Q4, LUNARA is the biggest revenue brand at €1.53B, but it is only at 47.5% of target. This makes it
the largest single contributor to the quarter gap.
1. Click the LUNARA bar. 2. Confirm how the KPI strip changes. 3. Open a brand drillthrough page. 4.
Decide whether the response is commercial, access, or execution focused.
When one brand contributes a disproportionate share of the variance, do not spread management attention
across the entire portfolio. First drill into that brand.

3. Decision pattern: stable share but weak target
attainment
The current slice shows 19.49% weighted market share with 60.8% attainment to target. When share remains
broadly stable while attainment falls sharply, leadership should test whether targets were stretched or whether
mix shifted toward lower-value channels or markets.
Questions to ask: Is the miss concentrated in a few brands or broad-based? Are large markets all below target,
or only one cluster? Do operational KPIs indicate poor execution, or does execution look acceptable relative to
plan?
Treat this pattern as a prioritization problem, not automatically as a market share emergency. Move next to the
scorecard and heat table.

4. Decision pattern: use the scorecard to identify the lever
Digital Channel Coverage is slightly above target, Target Coverage and Event Follow-up are close to target,
but Multi-Channel Engagement Score is severely under target. This is a classic signal that the organization is
achieving reach but not enough coordinated depth across channels.
1. Look for KPIs that are green. 2. Look for KPIs that are red but strategically important. 3. Compare one
red KPI with one near-target KPI to determine whether the next action should increase volume or improve
orchestration.
Increase multichannel choreography, revise field-force follow-up after events, or redesign high-priority account
plans instead of asking teams to “just do more calls”.

5. Decision pattern: prioritize markets with both size and
underperformance
Leadership should not chase the reddest color alone. The correct approach is to combine color with revenue
contribution. Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Italy, and Spain are all around 60% of target and together
represent the bulk of revenue in the quarter.
1. Sort the heat table by Revenue descending. 2. Select the first large red or orange market. 3. Check how
the brand chart changes under that country filter. 4. Compare the country scorecard with the portfolio
scorecard. 5. Escalate only after confirming whether the same issue repeats across multiple major markets.
By the end of the review, the user should be able to name the top two brands driving the miss, the top three
markets to prioritize, and the two KPI levers most likely to improve the next quarter.

1. Dashboard map: what is on the page
The executive page is a one-screen portfolio summary. It is built for BU Heads, Country Managers, and
Commercial Excellence leads who need a quick read on business performance before choosing a market,
brand, or quarter to investigate.
What is in the page
 The top header identifies the active reporting slice. In the mockup it is set to FY2025 Q4.
 The filter ribbon lets you limit the page by therapeutic area, brand, cluster, country, and fiscal quarter.
Those slicers should be configured to cross-filter every visual on the page.
 The KPI strip shows four executive cards: Revenue, % vs Target, Market Share, and YoY Growth. These
are the fastest indicators of business health.
 The left main visual is Revenue vs Target by Brand. It exposes which brands are driving the quarter and
which ones are creating the variance.
 The right main visual is Market Share Trend. It tells you whether the issue is a one-quarter event or part of
a longer pattern.
 The bottom row combines the KPI Scorecard and Country Performance Heat Table. Together they
answer: what operational drivers are weak, and where should leadership intervene first?

NovaCarta Pharma | Executive Dashboard Features & Navigation
Page 3
2. Filters and scope selection
In Power BI the page should open with global slicers at the top. Each slicer narrows the evaluation context for
all cards and charts on the page. A leadership user normally starts with fiscal quarter, then chooses a
geography, then optionally narrows to one therapeutic area or one brand.
How to change the scope step by step
1. Select Fiscal Quarter first. The executive page is most stable when one quarter is selected, because
targets are quarterly and the KPI scorecard in this dataset is stored at quarter level.
2. Select a Cluster or Country only after confirming the quarter. This prevents confusion between
time-driven changes and geography-driven changes.
3. Select Therapeutic Area when you want to compare portfolio behavior within a medical franchise, for
example Solid Tumors versus Neuroscience.
4. Select a Brand only when you intentionally want to collapse the page to a single-product view. Once a
brand is selected, the bar chart and heat table stop functioning as portfolio comparisons and become
distribution views.
5. Use the eraser icon on the slicer or the page reset button to return to the starting state after analysis.
If multiple countries are selected, the heat table should remain sorted by revenue so the largest markets stay
on top. If a brand is selected, page titles should update dynamically to avoid ambiguity.

3. KPI strip: how leadership reads the top row
The KPI strip is the triage layer. Leadership should be able to decide within seconds whether the quarter is on
plan, growing, or under pressure. In the FY2025 Q4 slice, the page shows Revenue of €3.70B, achievement of
60.8% to target, weighted market share of 19.49%, and weighted YoY growth of 19.62%.
How to use the cards
 Start with % vs Target. At 60.8%, the page immediately tells you the quarter is materially under plan.
 Use Revenue to size the problem. A 39.2% shortfall against a €6.08B target is a gap of €2.38B.
 Use Market Share and YoY Growth together. In this dummy slice, share remains close to 19.5% while
YoY growth is almost 19.6%. That means the business is still growing versus last year, even though it is
behind an aggressive plan.
 After reading the cards, go straight to brand contribution. Cards diagnose that a problem exists; the brand
chart explains where it comes from.
Cards themselves usually do not support drill-down. Instead, the user clicks a bar in the brand chart or a
country row in the heat table.

4. Main analysis visuals: brand contribution and market
trajectory
The central work of the executive page happens in the two large visuals. The left chart identifies the portfolio
components behind the variance. The right chart places the quarter in trend context.
How to use the bar chart
Read the bar chart from top to bottom. The first question is not “which brand is biggest?” but “which brand
contributes most to the target gap?” In FY2025 Q4, LUNARA contributes the largest absolute revenue but is
only at 47.5% of target, creating the largest variance.
How to use the trend chart
The market share chart tells you whether the under-performance is accompanied by structural share loss.
In this slice, weighted market share remains in a narrow band around 19.5% across twelve quarters.
1. Click a bar for one brand. The trend line should immediately recalculate for that selected brand only. 2.
Right-click and choose drillthrough to a Brand Detail page when you need more decomposition. 3. Ctrl-click
multiple bars when you want to compare a priority set.

5. Scorecard, heat table, and drill path
The bottom row converts the headline performance story into action. The KPI scorecard highlights which
operational signals are lagging. The country heat table localizes where the performance problem is most
material.
Reading the scorecard
Use the scorecard to distinguish between a demand problem and an execution problem. In the FY2025 Q4
example, Digital Channel Coverage is slightly above target, while Multi-Channel Engagement Score is only
30.6% of target. That means reach exists, but the quality and orchestration of engagement are weak.
Reading the heat table
The heat table shows that large markets such as Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Italy, and Spain all
sit around 60% to target. Because these are also the largest revenue contributors, they should be
addressed before small-market noise is investigated.
Start in the heat table by selecting one underperforming country, for example Germany. Then review the brand
bars under the Germany filter. Next, drill to the country page or brand page and inspect whether the issue is
concentrated in one franchise, one brand, or one execution KPI.

 Definitions & Calculations
The KPI logic behind the executive cards, scorecard, and heat table, with dataset-specific formulas
and worked examples.
Document purpose
This guide is designed for realistic end-user enablement on the Executive Performance Dashboard in the
NovaCarta EMEA dummy dataset. It explains what each area of the dashboard contains, how to filter and drill
into data, how the executive KPIs are calculated, and how to turn those signals into actions.
Reference quarter used for worked examples: FY2025 Q4. In that slice, total revenue is €3.70B, target
revenue is €6.08B, weighted market share is 19.49%, and weighted YoY growth is 19.62%.

NovaCarta Pharma | Executive Dashboard KPI Definitions & Calculations
Page 2
1. Revenue, target revenue, and revenue variance
Revenue is the sum of FACT_Sales[net_revenue_eur] in the active filter context. Target Revenue is the sum
of FACT_Targets[target_value] where target_type = REVENUE_TARGET in the same context.
Revenue = SUM(FACT_Sales[net_revenue_eur])
Revenue Target = CALCULATE(SUM(FACT_Targets[target_value]), FACT_Targets[target_type] =
"REVENUE_TARGET")
Revenue Variance = [Revenue] - [Revenue Target]
Worked example: Revenue = €3.70B; Revenue Target = €6.08B; Revenue Variance = €-2.38B.

NovaCarta Pharma | Executive Dashboard KPI Definitions & Calculations
Page 3
2. Revenue % vs Target
Revenue % vs Target converts the variance into a normalized attainment metric so geographies and quarters
become comparable even when the revenue base differs.
Revenue % vs Target = DIVIDE([Revenue], [Revenue Target])
Worked example: €3.70B ÷ €6.08B = 60.8%.
Interpretation: 100% means exactly on target, above 100% means over-delivery, below 100% means
under-delivery. At 60.8%, the miss is too large to ignore and must be decomposed by brand and country.

NovaCarta Pharma | Executive Dashboard KPI Definitions & Calculations
Page 4
3. Weighted Market Share and Weighted YoY Growth
The dataset stores market_share_pct and yoy_growth_pct at transaction level. The executive page uses
revenue-weighted averages so large sales records contribute proportionally.
Weighted Market Share = DIVIDE(SUMX(FACT_Sales, FACT_Sales[market_share_pct] *
FACT_Sales[net_revenue_eur]), SUM(FACT_Sales[net_revenue_eur]))
Weighted YoY Growth = DIVIDE(SUMX(FACT_Sales, FACT_Sales[yoy_growth_pct] *
FACT_Sales[net_revenue_eur]), SUM(FACT_Sales[net_revenue_eur]))
Worked example: weighted market share = 19.49%; weighted YoY growth = 19.62%.
